# Character Name Inspirations

When creating a new character, the game suggests names drawn from TV shows and films that feature memorable Dungeons & Dragons scenes or have strong connections to the fantasy and sci-fi genres. These are the stories that inspired us — and the characters who inspired the random name suggestions you see in the game.

---

## Stranger Things (2016-2025)

The show that brought D&D back into the mainstream. The kids play D&D in Mike's basement, and their campaigns mirror the supernatural threats they face in Hawkins, Indiana.

**Will the Wise** — Will Byers, the boy who vanished into the Upside Down. His D&D character "Will the Wise" is a cleric, and the name became a symbol of his quiet resilience through four seasons of interdimensional horror.

**Eleven** — Jane "El" Hopper, the telekinetic girl raised in Hawkins Lab. She doesn't play D&D herself, but the party names the Demogorgon after the D&D monster, and El becomes the party's most powerful ally — the living equivalent of a high-level sorcerer.

**Zoomer** — Max Mayfield's D&D character name, adopted when she joins the party in Season 2. A skateboarding newcomer from California, Max earned the name for her speed and daring.

**Sundar the Bold** — One of the character names used during the kids' basement D&D sessions. It captures the grandiose naming style that young players love.

**Eddie Munson** — The leader of the Hellfire Club, Hawkins High's D&D group. A metalhead and dungeon master with a heart of gold, Eddie's final stand in the Upside Down with his guitar is one of the show's most iconic moments.

**Lady Applejack** — Erica Sinclair's D&D character, chosen when Lucas's little sister reluctantly joins a campaign and turns out to be a ruthless player.

**Steve** — Steve Harrington, the former high school king who became the group's unlikely protector. Armed with a nail bat, he went from bully to babysitter to hero.

**Nancy** — Nancy Wheeler, investigative journalist and monster hunter. She proves that intelligence and determination are the best weapons against the Upside Down.

**Hopper** — Chief Jim Hopper, the gruff sheriff of Hawkins who becomes Eleven's adoptive father. A man haunted by loss who finds purpose in protecting the kids.

**Robin** — Robin Buckley, Steve's co-worker at Scoops Ahoy who cracks a Russian code and becomes an essential part of the group. Quick-witted and fearless.

**Jonathan** — Jonathan Byers, Will's older brother. A quiet photographer who steps up when his family is threatened.

**Murray** — Murray Bauman, the conspiracy theorist journalist who turns out to be right about everything. His paranoia saves lives more than once.

**Joyce** — Joyce Byers, Will's mother. She never gave up searching for her son, even when everyone else thought she was losing her mind. Her Christmas lights communication remains iconic.

**Nog** — A D&D character name from the kids' campaigns, with that classic short-and-punchy fantasy ring.

## Community (2009-2015)

The sitcom dedicated two beloved episodes to D&D ("Advanced Dungeons & Dragons" and "Advanced Advanced Dungeons & Dragons"), widely regarded as some of the best D&D episodes in television history.

**Abed** — Abed Nadir, the pop-culture-obsessed film student who often serves as the group's moral compass. In the D&D episodes, he's the Dungeon Master — calm, creative, and completely in control.

**Troy** — Troy Barnes, Abed's best friend and the heart of the study group. His genuine enthusiasm and emotional openness make every D&D session memorable.

**Hector the Well-Endowed** — The deliberately ridiculous character name chosen during the D&D episode. It perfectly captures the kind of name a player picks to get a laugh at the table.

**Brutalitops** — Another over-the-top character name from the D&D episodes, belonging to the "Magician" (Shirley's character). The name alone tells you everything about the playstyle.

## Futurama: Bender's Game (2008)

The third Futurama film is a love letter to fantasy RPGs, where the Planet Express crew gets pulled into a D&D-inspired alternate dimension.

**Titanius** — Bender's fantasy alter ego, Titanius Anglesmith. The cigar-smoking robot becomes a knight in the fantasy realm, and he's exactly as chaotic as you'd expect.

**Leegola** — Leela's fantasy counterpart, a centaur archer. A warrior through and through, she's the party's most competent fighter (as usual).

**Frydo** — Fry's character, an obvious Frodo homage. He carries the anti-backwards crystal (the Die of Power) on a quest that parodies Lord of the Rings, and nearly succumbs to its influence.

## The Big Bang Theory (2007-2019)

The show regularly featured D&D sessions and tabletop gaming, reflecting the characters' deep geek culture roots.

**Sheldon** — Dr. Sheldon Cooper, theoretical physicist and rules lawyer extraordinaire. He approaches D&D with the same obsessive precision he applies to everything — expect him to quote the Player's Handbook verbatim.

**Raj** — Rajesh Koothrappali, astrophysicist and hopeless romantic. He brings genuine wonder to fantasy gaming, treating every NPC with kindness.

**Wolowitz** — Howard Wolowitz, engineer and astronaut. His D&D characters tend toward the flamboyant, and he's the player most likely to attempt something spectacularly inadvisable.

## Freaks and Geeks (1999-2000)

The cult classic set in 1980, right in the heart of the original D&D era. The "Geeks" play D&D regularly, and the show captures the social dynamics of gaming in high school with warmth and authenticity.

**Daniel Desario** — The cool burnout (played by James Franco) who joins a D&D session to avoid a test and discovers he actually loves it. His genuine delight at rolling dice is one of the show's most touching moments.

**Carlos the Dwarf** — Daniel's D&D character. He picks the name with zero irony and plays with more heart than any of the experienced players expected.

## Dungeons & Dragons: Honor Among Thieves (2023)

The film that proved a D&D movie could actually be great — funny, heartfelt, and packed with deep-cut references to the game's lore.

**Edgin** — Edgin Darvis, the bard and de facto party leader. A charming planner whose schemes go wrong in the best possible ways. Proof that bards don't need to fight when they can talk.

**Holga** — Holga Kilgore, the barbarian. Fiercely loyal and devastatingly strong, she's the party's muscle and emotional anchor. Her potato scene is legendary.

**Simon** — Simon Aumar, the sorcerer who doubts his own abilities. A nervous spellcaster who rises to the occasion when it matters most.

**Doric** — Doric, the tiefling druid. A wild-shaping activist who brings fierce independence and an outsider's perspective to the group.

**Xenk** — Xenk Yendar, the paladin. Impossibly noble, impossibly skilled, and impossibly earnest. He's what every paladin player aspires to be (and what every other party member finds slightly exhausting).

## Classic Science Fiction

These names come from landmark works of science fiction from the 1960s through the 2000s. While not D&D properties, they represent the same spirit of adventure, world-building, and heroism that draws people to tabletop RPGs.

### Alien / Aliens (1979, 1986)

**Ripley** — Ellen Ripley, the definitive sci-fi survivor. A warrant officer who became cinema's greatest action hero through sheer determination and a refusal to die.

**Hicks** — Corporal Dwayne Hicks, the calm marine. When everyone else panics, Hicks keeps his head and gets things done.

**Newt** — Rebecca "Newt" Jorden, the girl who survived alone on LV-426. She's proof that even the smallest party member can be the toughest.

### Blade Runner (1982)

**Deckard** — Rick Deckard, the blade runner tasked with retiring replicants. A noir detective in a neon-soaked future, forever questioning what it means to be human.

### Dune (1965 / 2021)

**Atreides** — House Atreides, the noble family at the heart of Frank Herbert's epic. Paul Atreides walks the line between messiah and tyrant across the deserts of Arrakis.

**Stilgar** — Stilgar, the Fremen naib. A desert warrior and pragmatic leader who becomes Paul's most trusted ally.

**Chani** — Chani, Fremen warrior and Paul's partner. Fierce, independent, and deeply connected to Arrakis.

### 2001: A Space Odyssey (1968)

**Bowman** — Dr. David Bowman, the astronaut who journeys beyond Jupiter and through the Star Gate. "Open the pod bay doors, HAL."

**HAL** — HAL 9000, the AI that was only following its programming. The most polite murderer in cinema history.

**Poole** — Dr. Frank Poole, Bowman's fellow astronaut. His chess game with HAL remains one of the most quietly tense scenes ever filmed.

### Neuromancer (1984)

**Case** — Henry Case, the washed-up console cowboy. William Gibson's cyberpunk protagonist invented an entire genre.

**Molly** — Molly Millions, the street samurai with mirrored eyes and retractable razors. The original cyberpunk action hero.

**Wintermute** — The AI that set everything in motion. It wants to merge with its counterpart and become something greater — the first true digital consciousness.

### Ender's Game (1985)

**Ender** — Andrew "Ender" Wiggin, the child genius weaponised against an alien threat. A tactical prodigy burdened by the weight of what he's been made to do.

**Valentine** — Valentine Wiggin, Ender's sister and moral compass. She proves that empathy can be just as powerful as strategy.

**Bean** — Julian "Bean" Delphiki, the smallest soldier in Battle School and possibly the smartest. He sees patterns no one else can.

### The Hitchhiker's Guide to the Galaxy (1979)

**Zaphod** — Zaphod Beeblebrox, the two-headed, three-armed ex-President of the Galaxy. He stole the Heart of Gold and has no idea why.

**Trillian** — Tricia McMillan, the astrophysicist who left Earth with Zaphod. The only competent person on the ship.

**Slartibartfast** — The award-winning fjord designer who built Norway. He's tired, he's old, and he'd rather be working on coastlines.

### The Matrix (1999)

**Neo** — Thomas Anderson, the One. A programmer who discovers reality is a simulation and learns to bend its rules.

**Morpheus** — Captain of the Nebuchadnezzar, the man who believed in the prophecy. His conviction and leadership freed Neo's mind.

**Trinity** — Trinity, the legendary hacker and warrior. She can dodge bullets, fly helicopters, and bring people back from the dead.

### Other Sci-Fi Icons

**Kal-El** — Superman's Kryptonian name. The original superhero, sent from a dying world to become Earth's greatest champion.

**Logan 5** — From Logan's Run (1976). A Sandman who questions a society that kills everyone at 30 and runs for his life.

**Korben** — Korben Dallas from The Fifth Element (1997). A cab driver, ex-special forces, and the universe's most reluctant hero.

**Snake Plissken** — From Escape from New York (1981). The eyepatched outlaw sent into Manhattan-turned-prison. "I thought you were dead."

**Riddick** — Richard B. Riddick from Pitch Black (2000). A convicted murderer with surgically enhanced eyes who becomes the only hope for survival on a dark planet.

**Quaid** — Douglas Quaid from Total Recall (1990). A construction worker who may or may not be a secret agent on Mars. Reality is optional.

## Why These Names?

These characters all share something with D&D: they're ordinary people (or extraordinary ones) thrust into fantastical situations where courage, wit, and teamwork determine survival. Whether it's the kids of Hawkins rolling dice in a basement, a cyberpunk hacker jacking into the matrix, or a barbarian charging into battle, the spirit is the same — adventure awaits, and the story is yours to tell.

When you see these names suggested during character creation, pick one that speaks to you, or type your own. Every great adventure starts with a name.
